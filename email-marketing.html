Challenge

A seasonal product, a year-round list.

Firewood demand spikes with the weather, but the email list doesn't take the summer off. The program needed a steady, recognizable cadence that stays relevant in every season — cook-outs, holidays, the first cold night — without wearing the list out.

Strategy

Timely moments, one clear ask.

  • Calendar-driven subject lines tied to real moments: Labor Day fires, a Halloween fire-pit night, “October is one week away,” “How's the stack holding up?”
  • Two audiences: homeowners, plus a restaurant angle (“Autumn service starts soon. Is your wood supply sorted?”).
  • One CTA per email — shop or reorder kiln-dried firewood with free delivery.
  • A recognizable look: a reusable header system so every send reads as Axe & Kindle at a glance.
My Role

Every step, every send.

  • Plan the email calendar alongside the social calendar.
  • Write subject lines, preheaders and body copy.
  • Design headers and imagery in Canva for seasonal campaigns.
  • Build, schedule and send broadcasts in Drip; review results and list health.
Execution

From calendar to campaign.

  • Seasonal planning: connect email topics with holidays, cold-weather preparation and live-fire cooking.
  • Creative production: write copy and design branded headers, then build and schedule broadcasts in Drip.
  • Audience relevance: pair homeowner lifestyle messages with restaurant supply and reorder angles.
  • Reporting: review sends, opens, clicks, unsubscribes and bounces to identify opportunities for the next campaign.

Results

Read the results, then prioritize.

The historical email analytics slide reports a 15% open rate and 0.41% click-through rate in 2023, compared with 10% and 0.29% in 2024. Over the same years, the reported unsubscribe rate declined from 0.33% to 0.13% and the bounce rate from 0.1% to 0.05%.

Lower unsubscribes and bounces sit alongside weaker opens and clicks: the opportunity is to improve message relevance and engagement. These are program-level observations, not proof that a particular design caused a change. No email-attributed revenue or conversion lift is claimed.

Source: Email Analytics, slide 14 of my Axe & Kindle + Every Window analytics presentation. The 2025 row is an interim snapshot from the 2025 review, not a full-year comparison. Historical figures are separate from the creative examples shown here.

Key Takeaways

What I'd carry forward.

  1. Keep seasonal messaging specific to the customer's next use: a fireplace, a grill or a restaurant service.
  2. Test subject lines and calls to action while monitoring clicks and list health together.
  3. Prioritize segmentation, welcome and cart-recovery flows as future opportunities; measure their effect before claiming results.
