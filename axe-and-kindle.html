Lifestyle e-commerce · Social + Email

Making firewood feel like a ritual.

Kiln-dried firewood is a practical purchase. My job was to make it feel like the start of something — a fire-lit evening, a cook-out, a slow weekend — and give people a reason to save, share and order.

Challenge

A commodity that has to feel premium.

Axe & Kindle sells kiln-dried hickory, oak and mixed hardwood with free nationwide delivery. Online, firewood competes with “I'll grab some at the gas station.” The brand needed content that explains why kiln-dried wood is worth it, keeps the account active through the seasons, and earns trust from people who have never bought firewood online.

Strategy

Lifestyle first, then proof.

  • Lead with the moment, not the log. Fireside evenings, holidays and live-fire cooking carry the emotion; the product shows up as the reason the moment works.
  • Teach in carousels. Short, swipeable explainers (hickory vs. oak, the top-down fire, what “kiln-dried” really means) built to be saved.
  • Borrow attention responsibly. Curate standout fire-cooking Reels from creators, always credited, to reach new people.
  • Close with proof and a clear ask. Real customer reviews, consistent end slides and a single CTA: order at axeandkindle.com, free delivery.
My Role

Planner, designer, writer, publisher.

I own Axe & Kindle's social and email execution end to end:

  • Build the monthly content calendar and seasonal themes.
  • Design carousels, statics, Stories and email headers in Canva, using AI image generation for scene photography and finishing everything by hand.
  • Write captions, hashtags and CTAs in the brand's premium-rustic voice (“seasoned wood,” never “wet wood”).
  • Source and credit creator Reels; schedule posts in Buffer; send campaigns in Drip; review results in Sprout Social.
Verified: all 152 Axe & Kindle Instagram feed posts in the Sprout export list me as the sender.
Execution

A repeatable system, season after season.

  • Carousel formula: 5–6 slides — a hook cover, numbered steps, and a branded “Firewood, perfected.” end slide with the delivery offer.
  • Seasonal bridge: calendars that roll from summer cook-outs into fall fires and holiday hosting (Friendsgiving, gifting, “skip the crowds” on Black Friday).
  • Email: a library of 69 email header designs covering every season and holiday, from National Firewood Day to Father's Day.
  • Social proof: customer review cards built from real customer quotes.
  • Channel mix: Instagram, Facebook and a restaurant- and hospitality-focused LinkedIn angle.

Key Takeaways

What I'd carry forward.

  1. Earn the save or the share before asking for the sale — moments and how-tos outperform product shots.
  2. Curated video reaches people; original carousels give them something to keep. A healthy calendar needs both, clearly credited.
  3. A strict template (hook → steps → branded end slide) is what makes a one-person team consistent across months.
  4. Next step: tie social and email to site traffic and orders so content decisions can be judged on revenue, not just engagement.
